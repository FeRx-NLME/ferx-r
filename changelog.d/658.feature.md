- **ferx now builds against ferx-core `32dbb309`**, up from `81468734`
  (#658). These engine changes reach R:

  - **`covariance_method = "s"` and `"rsr"` work on a fit with a `profile`d
    theta level block**
    ([ferx-core #2108](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2108),
    [#2403](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2403)). Both were
    refused with `E_PROFILE_SCOPE`, by `ferx_fit()` and by
    `ferx_covariance()` (#640). `rsr` is the unprofiled sandwich with the
    profiled R^-1; `s` is S^-1 over every parameter, levels included, and
    still needs more subjects than free parameters (on `mbma_placebo`'s six
    subjects S is singular and the step reports `covariance_failed`).
    `ferx_covariance()` repeats either step bit for bit, also after
    `ferx_save_fit()` / `ferx_load_fit()`.
  - **New warning `W_COV_RSR_ONE_SUBJECT_LEVELS`**
    ([ferx-core #2415](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2415)): an
    `rsr` step on a model with a theta level block, profiled or not, says
    when some levels are each read by a single subject, as every
    `[STUDY, TIME]` level of `mbma_placebo` is. Those levels' `rsr` standard
    errors are not usable (1.5 to 4.8 times below `r`'s there).
    `ferx_get_warnings()` says the other `rsr` standard errors stand and to
    read those levels' from `covariance_method = "r"`.
  - **A `profile`d level block may declare bounds**, `theta PLACEBO[STUDY,
    TIME](0.0, -10.0, 4.0) profile`
    ([ferx-core #2112](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2112),
    [#2402](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2402),
    [#2416](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2416)); it was
    refused with `E_PROFILE_SCOPE`. The levels are the exact minimiser over
    the box. A level that ends on its bound is held there, reported in a
    `boundary_estimate` warning, and held by the covariance step as a FIX
    theta is: the new `fit$profiled_levels_at_bound` names those levels,
    `print()` shows their SE as `N/A`, `fit$estimates` and `fit$theta_levels`
    give them an `NA` `se`, and every other standard error is computed with
    them held. `ferx_covariance()` and `ferx_save_fit()` / `ferx_load_fit()`
    keep the list. On `mbma_placebo` with that box the profiled fit's OFV is
    6.5e-9 below the unprofiled fit with the same bounds.
  - **A profiled fit's structural covariance stencil steps at `3e-3` by
    default**, not `fd_hessian_step = 1e-2`
    ([ferx-core #2414](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2414)),
    bringing its standard errors about ten times closer to the exact ones.
    An explicit `fd_hessian_step` is used as written. At the smaller step a
    profiled fit with an exactly confounded pair of thetas can now report
    standard errors where its covariance step used to fail; the pair still
    shows a correlation of -1.00 and a high condition number. Profiled
    standard errors change for fits that did not set `fd_hessian_step`.
  - **A profiled block solves its levels on the exact analytic gradient**
    wherever the outer gradient is analytic
    ([ferx-core #2109](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2109),
    [#2401](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2401)): the same
    estimates and OFV, about twice as fast on a 100-study placebo model. A
    subject the adjoint cannot serve falls back to finite differences and is
    named in a warning.
  - **A profiled fit with `optimizer = bobyqa` is converged only once its
    tolerance stop is polished**
    ([ferx-core #2336](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2336),
    [#2399](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2399)), as an
    unprofiled BOBYQA fit is. Such fits had stopped as converged up to 32 OFV
    above the optimum; they now cost about 2.4 times the objective
    evaluations.
  - **`ferx_fit(inits_from_nca = ...)` / `ferx_inits_from_nca()` route each
    NCA estimate reproducibly, and only to a parameter that has a theta**
    ([ferx-core #1930](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/1930),
    [#2397](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/pull/2397)). A parameter's
    theta is now found through the mu-reference on its own line, so the same
    model on the same data no longer starts from different values on
    different runs. When two random effects point one parameter at
    different thetas, nothing is written for it and a warning names both. A
    parameter bound to a literal or to `TIME` is no longer matched to a theta
    by name.
