- **Results and output that change with ferx-core `a8f5f056..13e6c8bf`** (#777):

  - **An `omega NAME ~ 0 FIX` placeholder eta is removed from the model**, as
    NONMEM holds `$OMEGA 0 FIX`
    ([ferx-core #3150](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/3150)).
    It used to stay a full dimension of every subject's inner problem at a
    variance of `1e-8`, slowing the fit and moving the OFV by about 3e-6. Now
    the fit is the model written without it, bit for bit, and the new
    `W_ZERO_FIX_ETA_DROPPED` note names what was removed. The removed eta has
    no row in `fit$omega`, `fit$eta_names`, the EBEs, the shrinkage tables or
    the `sdtab`. An omega or a table of etas supplied for the declared shape is
    refused (`E_SUPPLIED_ETAS` for etas). Seeded `saem`, `imp`, `impmap`,
    `bayes` and `vi` fits, and seeded simulations, draw a different stream on
    such a model, since the stream follows the eta count. An all-zero
    `block_omega ... FIX` goes the same way. A placeholder read by
    `iiv_on_ruv`, a `[mixture]` override, an endpoint block or FREM stays.
  - **An adaptive run with two monitors of the same name is refused**
    (`E_ADAPTIVE_OPTIONS`, naming the monitor;
    [ferx-core #946](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/946)).
    Before, the controller silently read only the last one.
  - **`ferx_vpc_tte()`, `ferx_vpc_rtte()` and `ferx_vpc_kmmc()` refuse an
    empty `grid`** with `E_VPC_TTE_INPUT`, where they returned every stratum
    with no grid
    ([ferx-core #2947](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2947)).
  - **A Kaplan-Meier VPC curve is undefined past its last time also where it
    has reached 0**
    ([ferx-core #2930](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2930)).
    `band$obs` and the band are now `NA` there, in `ferx_vpc_tte()` and
    `ferx_vpc_rtte()`; they were 0, which held the band's lower edge at 0 late
    in the curve. A late band point is `NA` where too few replicates are still
    followed up, and elsewhere it can rise.
  - **Endpoint rows and missing-`DV` rows are records of the PK model**, as in
    NONMEM
    ([ferx-core #2368](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2368)): a
    time-to-event, count or discrete-state row, and an observation row skipped
    for its missing `DV` (`W_MISSING_DV`), now govern the interval ending at
    them with their covariates and occasion, can govern an `ADDL`-implied dose,
    and a leading missing-`DV` row starts the integration clock. Joint models
    whose covariates change at such a row fit differently. Under nested levels
    such a row with a blank level cell is refused (`E_LEVEL_MISSING_LABEL`). A
    saved fit's population fingerprint goes stale.
  - **SDE (`[diffusion]`) models score each row's own residual variance in the
    Kalman filter**
    ([ferx-core #3126](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/3126)): a
    custom magnitude, `weight =`, a `power(...)` exponent, `block_sigma` and
    `iiv_on_ruv` now reach the filter, where it used the bare `sigma` for every
    row, so such fits score a different (correct) likelihood. `block_sigma`
    with `[diffusion]` takes finite-difference gradients for now, so
    `optimizer = "auto"` picks BOBYQA there.
  - **Per-subject OFV contributions sum to the OFV under Laplace and adaptive
    quadrature**
    ([ferx-core #3149](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/3149)):
    under `method = "laplace"`, `focei` with `n_agq > 1` and `agq_eval_only`,
    the `sdtab` column `EBE_OFV` is now the subject's own quadrature term; it
    was the post-hoc FOCEI score.
  - **`[derived]` compartment reads on a closed-form transit / IG model are
    `NaN`** for a subject its ODE equivalent serves (a steady-state or infusion
    dose, a `TIME`-reading parameter, flip-flop kinetics), with the new
    `W_DERIVED_CMT_TWIN_ANALYTICAL` warning; they reported the equivalent's
    states under the wrong names
    ([ferx-core #1580](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/1580)).
  - **A steady-state warning only when the estimates have no steady state**
    ([ferx-core #1522](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/1522)):
    "Steady-state (SS=1) equilibration did not converge" now comes from the pass
    at the reported estimates, not from trial points of the search, the
    covariance step or SIR; a multi-start fit reports the winning start's.
  - **Fits that move to a better or verified optimum**: L-BFGS and SLSQP scale
    their first leg consistently, so a one-state turnover model no longer
    collapses onto its guard while reporting converged, and every default
    L-BFGS fit with a capped first gradient takes a slightly different path
    ([ferx-core #3153](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/3153)); a
    restart that reached the optimum with a small step is verified by a
    perturbed restart and reported converged
    ([ferx-core #2707](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2707)); a
    `[mixture]` fit's L-BFGS stop is verified at a tight inner tolerance and is
    no longer reported converged when that fails
    ([ferx-core #2745](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2745)); the
    inner screen descends from spread-out starts among its 32 lowest points
    ([ferx-core #2863](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2863)); a
    finite-difference EBE gradient on an adaptively integrated ODE model uses a
    step the integrator can resolve
    ([ferx-core #2868](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2868),
    [#3145](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/3145)); and a subject
    whose first record is `EVID=3` or `EVID=4` multi-starts as with `EVID=1`
    ([ferx-core #2081](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2081)).
  - **Adaptive `round_to` no longer moves a dose that is already on the grid**
    at very fine grids
    ([ferx-core #2927](https://github.com/FeRx-NLME/ferx-core/issues/2927)); a
    tie reached by a decrease of more than about 95% at a non-integer
    percentage can round down, and the `W_ADAPTIVE_STEP_ABSORBED` threshold for
    a tiny `increase N%` moves accordingly.
